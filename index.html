01 · 15 min

Handling a Customer Request

Imagine you are a customer support agent. A customer has contacted you twice because their service request has not been resolved. Record a 2–3 minute response as if you were speaking directly to them.

Customer: “I submitted my request on Monday. Yesterday I was told someone would contact me, but I haven't heard anything. I need this sorted out before Friday. Can you tell me what's happening?”

Include all five elements: acknowledge the inconvenience; summarize the issue; explain what you can verify versus what is still uncertain; offer a realistic next step; and explain how you will follow up. Use at least two modals and two professional expressions.

03 · 12 min

A Delayed Service Request

Read the case file carefully. Some answers require you to connect information across paragraphs or infer why a particular response is appropriate. Then answer all 10 questions.

Case File: A Delayed Service Request

To: Customer Support Team
From: Priya Shah, Service Operations
Subject: SR-4821 | Account access request | Thursday, 10:15 AM

On Monday morning, customer Daniel Reed submitted a request to restore access to his online account after changing his phone. The first agent confirmed his identity and forwarded the case to the technical team. An automated email told Daniel that most requests are reviewed within two business days, but it did not guarantee a resolution date.

On Tuesday afternoon, the technical team found that the new phone number had not been fully verified. They asked the support team to request an additional security check. Unfortunately, the note was added to the internal case file but no one contacted Daniel. When he called again on Wednesday, an agent told him someone would follow up shortly, without checking whether the additional verification had been completed.

Daniel has now called for the third time. He is frustrated because he needs access before a payment deadline on Friday. The case is still marked Pending Customer Verification, although Daniel does not know that a verification step is required. The technical team can continue once the identity check is completed, but it cannot promise that access will be restored before Friday.

Priya has assigned the current agent three tasks: explain the outstanding verification step without requesting sensitive details through an insecure channel; send Daniel the approved verification instructions; and document a follow-up for 3:00 PM today. If verification is completed, the agent should notify the technical team and provide a new status update. If it is not completed, the agent should explain what is still needed and when the next update can be expected.

Priya reminds the team: “Acknowledge the inconvenience, distinguish what has happened from what may happen next, and never guarantee a deadline unless the responsible team has confirmed it.”

04 · 15 min

An Escalated Customer Call

Audio supplied by your trainer

Listen to a customer and an agent discussing an unresolved account-access request. First, identify the problem and the customer's concern. On your second listen, focus on what is confirmed, what is uncertain, and what the agent promises to do. Answer the questions before opening the transcript.

Reveal transcript after listening

Agent: Thank you for calling Northgate Support. My name is Taylor. How may I help you today?

Customer: I’ve already called twice about my account access. I was told someone would get back to me, but nobody did. I have a payment due tomorrow, and this delay is becoming a serious problem.

Agent: I understand why you’re frustrated, especially with a deadline approaching. I’m sorry we haven’t kept you updated. May I take a moment to review the notes on your request?

Customer: Yes, but I don’t want another vague promise. Can you actually fix it today?

Agent: I can confirm that the technical team reviewed your request on Tuesday. They identified an additional verification step, but it appears we didn’t explain that to you. I apologize for the lack of communication.

Customer: So you’re saying I have to do something else? Why didn’t anyone tell me yesterday?

Agent: You should have been informed earlier. I can send the approved verification instructions through our secure channel now. Once you complete that step, I’ll notify the technical team immediately.

Customer: If I do it this morning, will I definitely have access by tomorrow?

Agent: I don’t want to give you an inaccurate guarantee. Completing verification will allow the team to continue, but the restoration time hasn’t been confirmed. What I can promise is a status update by 3:00 PM today, even if the issue is still being investigated.

Customer: All right. Could you explain where I’ll find those instructions?

Agent: Of course. Please sign in to the secure customer portal using your usual address. You should see a message titled “Additional Verification Required.” Do not send your password or verification code by email.

Customer: I can do that. What happens if the message doesn’t appear?

Agent: Please let us know through the secure support form. We can check whether the message was delivered and arrange another approved method. I’ll also document our conversation so you won’t have to repeat everything on your next call.

Customer: Thank you. I’m still disappointed, but at least I know what needs to happen.

Agent: That’s understandable. I’m sorry for the inconvenience, and I appreciate the opportunity to help move this forward.

Shadowing practice

Replay the audio and shadow these five phrases. Focus on empathetic intonation, sentence stress and natural rhythm.

  1. I understand why you're frustrated, especially with a deadline approaching.
  2. May I take a moment to review the notes on your request?
  3. I don't want to give you an inaccurate guarantee.
  4. What I can promise is a status update by 3:00 PM today.
  5. I'll also document our conversation so you won't have to repeat everything.
05 · 10 min

Write a Customer Follow-Up Email

Your role: You are a customer support agent. You have just spoken with Daniel, who has called three times because he still cannot access his online account.

Customer case: What you know

  • Daniel changed his phone and requested account access on Monday.
  • The technical team reviewed the request, but an additional identity verification is still required.
  • Daniel has not completed the verification yet because no one explained the next step.
  • He needs access before Friday, but the restoration date is not confirmed.
  • You can send the approved instructions through the secure customer portal.
  • You will provide a status update by 3:00 PM today, even if the issue remains unresolved.

Your task: Write the email you would send to Daniel after the call. Your goal is to rebuild trust, explain the current status, and make the next steps easy to understand.

Follow this 5-part structure

  1. Greeting + empathy: Address Daniel and acknowledge the frustrating delay.
  2. Current status: Explain that the case is waiting for additional verification.
  3. Customer action: Ask him politely to follow the instructions in the secure portal.
  4. Your commitment: Explain what you can do next and promise an update by 3:00 PM today.
  5. Closing: Thank him for his patience and end professionally.

Write 90–120 words. Include at least one appropriate modal (can, could, may, should, must). Do not guarantee account access before Friday or request passwords or verification codes by email.

Need help getting started? See a sentence starter

Dear Daniel, thank you for speaking with me today. I understand how frustrating this delay has been, especially with your payment deadline approaching...

Continue in your own words. Do not copy a complete model email.
